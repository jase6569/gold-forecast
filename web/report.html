UK Gold Forecast: what actually works

Stage one backtest, generated 18 November 2026

The short version

Tested on 5,771 trading days of gold in pounds, December 2003 to November 2026. History was split at October 2015: an indicator only counts if it worked in both halves.

5 of 22 classic trader signals passed that test: rsi(2) under 10 bounces, over 90 pulls back, below lower bollinger band bounces, above upper pulls back, stochastic under 20 bounces, over 80 pulls back, a down day is followed by an up day (and vice versa), down day while above the 200-day average: buy the dip.

The combined model called tomorrow's direction right 53.1% of the time, against 52.3% for simply saying "up" every day. On its strong-signal days (1,735 of them) it was right 55.9%.

Looking a week ahead (useful for "hold until it goes up"), it called the 5-day direction right 53.6% of the time, against 53.8% for always saying up.

For selling: on days it flagged "sell tomorrow", gold moved -0.099% the next day on average. On "hold" days it moved +0.091%. The average day was +0.035%.

What this means for the app

Only signals that passed here go into the evening forecast. The app shows a firm "sell tomorrow" or "hold" on strong-signal days and says "no clear call" the rest of the time, rather than guessing.
